# Treatment (proposed)

## The idea

**专家不是脑子更大，是组块更多。**

The piece argues one counter-intuitive claim: what separates an expert from a beginner is mostly not
raw intelligence but how much they can hold at once, because they stopped seeing single pieces and
started seeing groups. It lands on something the viewer can act on tonight.

Why this angle: it is genuinely counter-intuitive to a student, it is entirely graphic (scattered
pieces condensing into groups is an animatable idea with no footage), and it speaks to someone
learning to program, where "I can read every line but I cannot see the shape" is a real experience.

## Trace

1. **Cold open.** Many identical marks drifting, uncountable. The arithmetic of why you cannot hold them.
2. **The experiment.** 1946, a Dutch chess master shows masters and novices real board positions.
   Masters recall nearly everything. This is the passage everyone quotes.
3. **The reversal.** The same masters, shown *random* positions, are no better than novices. The
   advantage was never memory capacity.
4. **The mechanism.** The pieces are not stored one by one; they are stored as groups. Two, three,
   five pieces become one thing. Working memory holds the groups.
5. **The landing.** What "learning" actually is: not accumulating facts, but repeatedly re-seeing
   the same material until pieces collapse into chunks. What to do differently.
6. **Close.** A short statement and a source line.

## Look and sound

Same field as the reference: near-black blue ground, light type, one warm accent, a persistent bottom
text line, continuous motion that never resets to black. Beats at 112.35 BPM carry the accents
(piece counts, reveals, the reversal), never every onset.

## Honest limits

The reversing half of the story needs care. The chess study is real and widely reported; the exact
figures vary between retellings, so the film states the shape of the finding, not invented numbers,
and closes with a source line.

The visual reading of the reference was measured, not seen: this session cannot view images, so the
look is inferred from palette, brightness, motion and band measurements. The user is the eyes on
the result.
